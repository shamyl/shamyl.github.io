## Space Tech

**SpaceX Starship Achieves Historic Intact Splashdown**
SpaceX conducted its 13th Starship test flight on July 24, achieving a major milestone with the first intact splashdown of the vehicle in the Indian Ocean. The Starship V3 successfully deployed 20 Starlink V3 satellites and performed an in-space engine restart before gently tipping over and floating in the ocean west of Australia. This marks a critical step toward tower capture on the next flight and eventual orbital refueling demonstrations for NASA's Artemis program.

**NASA Deep Space Network Evacuated Due to Wildfire**
A fast-moving wildfire in Spain forced the evacuation of NASA's Madrid Deep Space Communications Complex on July 24. The tracking station, part of the global Deep Space Network supporting over 40 missions including Artemis and Voyager, suspended operations as flames approached the facility. The DSN provides crucial communications coverage as Earth's rotation brings spacecraft in and out of view.

**NASA Programs Feel Workforce Reduction Impact**
More than two dozen major NASA projects report being affected by the loss of approximately 20% of the agency's civil servant workforce over the past year. Programs including Orion, SLS, and DaVinci are experiencing delays and challenges as they adapt to reduced staffing levels.

**China's Long March 3B Survives Lightning Strike**
In a dramatic display of engineering resilience, a Chinese Long March 3B rocket was struck by lightning seconds after liftoff on July 23 but still successfully delivered its payload to orbit. The mission launched the Tianlian-2 06 data relay satellite from the Xichang Satellite Launch Center.

**EU Restricts Satellite Imagery Over Iran War Region**
At the request of the United States, the European Union has implemented a 24-hour delay on releasing Copernicus Sentinel-1 and Sentinel-2 satellite imagery over the Gulf of Oman and Strait of Hormuz region. The restriction coincides with renewed US military operations in the area.
