Pakistan at LEAP 2026: Digital Stack, Saudi Tech Ties and What the Largest-Ever Delegation Means for IT Exports

#pakistan#LEAP-2026#IT-exports#digital-nation#Saudi-Arabia#tech-policy

Pakistan mounted its largest-ever technology delegation at LEAP 2026 in Riyadh this week, with Federal IT Minister Shaza Fatima Khawaja unveiling a national digital stack anchored in the Digital Nation Pakistan Act. The pavilion featured 25 startups and 50 technology companies, a cybersecurity MoU was signed with Saudi Arabia, and Pakistani firms reported measurable commercial outcomes from previous editions. Taken together, the week signals that Pakistan is attempting to convert policy reform and conference participation into actual technology export revenue.

In Brief

  • Pakistan’s pavilion at LEAP 2026 showcased 25 startups and 50 tech companies through the Tech Destination Pakistan Pavilion, organized by PSEB and TDAP
  • Federal IT Minister Shaza Fatima Khawaja announced a national digital stack underpinned by the Digital Nation Pakistan Act, including 5G spectrum auctions, Special Technology Zones, and reduced IT taxation
  • Pakistan and Saudi Arabia signed a cybersecurity cooperation MoU during the event
  • PSEB reported that Pakistan’s 2025 LEAP presence — over 100 companies and 1,500 delegates — generated $9.74 million in business and 78 qualified leads
  • Companies like ByteCorp, KAACIB, Fortify, and Stellisys are moving from attending conferences to establishing physical offices in Saudi Arabia

What Was Announced at LEAP 2026

LEAP 2026, held in Riyadh, is described by its organizers as the world’s largest technology event. According to Digital Pakistan, this year’s gathering brought together more than 1,800 technology firms, 600 startups, 1,900 investors, and over 1,000 speakers.

Pakistan’s presence was spearheaded by the Tech Destination Pakistan Pavilion, which showcased 25 startups and 50 technology companies working on AI, automation, and cloud solutions. The pavilion was designed to facilitate B2B deals, attract foreign investment, and broaden market access for Pakistani technology exports, according to Digital Pakistan’s reporting on September 3, 2026.

Federal Minister for IT and Telecommunication Shaza Fatima Khawaja delivered a keynote titled “How We Built a Digital Nation,” in which she announced that Pakistan is developing a national digital stack. According to Digital Pakistan, Khawaja said the effort is anchored in the Digital Nation Pakistan Act and positioned Pakistan as “one of the region’s most attractive investment destinations.”

The minister highlighted several specific reforms: one of the country’s largest 5G spectrum auctions, the creation of Special Technology Zones, and reduced taxation for IT firms. She argued that a dedicated central digital agency with strict, measurable KPIs is essential to translate policy into delivery.

Separately, Pakistan and Saudi Arabia signed a memorandum of understanding on cybersecurity cooperation during the event. The MoU was reported by the Saudi Press Agency on September 1, 2026, and by Radio Pakistan and the Express Tribune on September 2-3, 2026.

From Booths to Business: The Commercial Track Record

The most significant data point from LEAP is not a speech — it is a number. According to PSEB data reported by Digital Pakistan on August 31, 2026, Pakistan’s LEAP 2025 delegation generated $9.74 million in business and 78 qualified leads. That delegation included over 100 companies and 1,500 delegates.

For LEAP 2026, the PSEB and TDAP-supported pavilion includes 20 companies, with several other firms attending independently. The shift from 100+ companies in 2025 to a curated 20-company official pavilion in 2026 suggests a focus on quality over quantity — targeting firms with specific Saudi market ambitions rather than broad attendance.

Several companies reported concrete progress:

  • ByteCorp Technologies, led by CEO Abdul Muqsit Abbasi, described LEAP as improving with each edition and credited deep industry connections for opening doors in the Kingdom.
  • KAACIB, whose founding member Khizer Ahmed Siddiqui said the 2025 event helped the company secure a local base, is now working to set up its Riyadh office.
  • Fortify, a property tech startup, sees alignment with Saudi Arabia’s rapidly developing real estate sector and Vision 2030 priorities, according to founder Syed Zain Ali.
  • Stellisys, developed by FifthThought, introduced a Saudi-specific version of its ERP platform that includes ZATCA-compliant invoicing, an AI layer, and tools to migrate businesses from older systems without losing historical data.
  • MaalBardaar brought a digital logistics platform designed to give importers and exporters full visibility across supply chains.

This progression — from attending to exhibiting to setting up local offices — is the kind of pipeline that converts conference participation into export revenue.

The Digital Nation Pakistan Act: What We Know

The Digital Nation Pakistan Act appears to be the legislative backbone of the digital stack announced at LEAP. According to the Nation (Pakistan), Federal IT Minister Khawaja briefed Sindh CM Murad Ali Shah on the Act on August 29, 2026.

Based on Khawaja’s LEAP keynote and subsequent reporting, the Act’s framework includes:

ComponentWhat It Covers
National Digital StackCore digital infrastructure built by the state, with private sector leading innovation
Central Digital AgencyA dedicated body with measurable KPIs to translate policy into delivery
5G Spectrum AuctionOne of the country’s largest, aimed at improving connectivity infrastructure
Special Technology ZonesGeographic zones with incentives for technology companies
IT Tax ReformReduced taxation for IT firms to improve competitiveness

The Act’s emphasis on a central digital agency with KPIs is notable. Pakistan’s technology policy implementation has historically suffered from fragmented institutional ownership — multiple ministries, authorities, and boards with overlapping mandates. A centralized agency with delivery targets, if properly empowered, could address this structural weakness.

However, the specifics of the Act — its legislative status, implementation timeline, budget allocation, and enforcement mechanisms — are not yet fully public. The gap between announcement and detailed implementation will determine whether this becomes operational infrastructure or remains a conference talking point.

The Saudi Arabia Connection

Pakistan’s technology relationship with Saudi Arabia deepened meaningfully at LEAP 2026. Three developments stand out:

First, the cybersecurity MoU. The Saudi Press Agency reported the signing on September 1, 2026. While the specific terms are not yet public, cybersecurity cooperation aligns with Saudi Arabia’s Vision 2030 priorities and Pakistan’s growing interest in digital governance and data protection.

Second, the commercial pipeline. The PSEB data on the 2025 delegation — $9.74 million in business and 78 qualified leads — represents a measurable, if modest, return. Pakistan’s IT exports reached a record $4.6 billion in FY2025-26, as covered previously. Saudi-bound exports are a small fraction of that total today, but the growth trajectory and the establishment of physical offices by Pakistani firms in Riyadh suggest the relationship is moving from exploratory to operational.

Third, ministerial-level engagement. According to the Express Tribune (September 2, 2026), Khawaja also used the platform to reiterate Islamabad’s intent to deepen collaboration with Saudi Arabia in digital innovation and capacity building. Dawn reported (September 2, 2026) that the IT minister showcased Pakistan’s digital transformation at the summit. The Nation (September 1, 2026) reported that Pakistan and Saudi Arabia agreed to strengthen cooperation in AI and data governance.

Why This Matters

For Pakistan’s technology sector, LEAP 2026 matters on three levels.

At the export level, Saudi Arabia represents one of the most accessible expansion markets for Pakistani technology firms. The Kingdom is investing heavily in digital infrastructure under Vision 2030, has a large and growing demand for technology services, and shares cultural and geographic proximity with Pakistan. Pakistani firms that establish local offices — as KAACIB is doing — are positioning themselves for multi-year contracts, not one-off deals.

At the policy level, the Digital Nation Pakistan Act and the national digital stack represent an attempt to build the domestic infrastructure that makes technology exports credible. A country pitching digital services globally needs its own digital infrastructure to be functional. The emphasis on a central digital agency with KPIs, if implemented, addresses one of the chronic weaknesses in Pakistan’s technology governance.

At the ecosystem level, the progression from broad participation (100+ companies in 2025) to curated, outcome-focused attendance (20 companies with specific Saudi market strategies in 2026) indicates maturation. The firms going to LEAP are increasingly going with products — Saudi-specific ERP platforms, ZATCA-compliant invoicing, logistics visibility tools — rather than profiles.

For Pakistani Founders and Technology Teams

For technology founders considering Saudi expansion, the LEAP pipeline offers a practical template:

  1. Attend first to understand the market. Abbasi’s advice — that LEAP offers the best environment for new entrants to understand how the Saudi market functions — is consistent with what most cross-border expansion strategies recommend.
  2. Build Saudi-specific adaptations. Stellisys did not just show up with its existing ERP; it built a ZATCA-compliant version. That kind of localization is what converts interest into contracts.
  3. Use PSEB and TDAP support. The official pavilion provides infrastructure, credibility, and access that independent attendance cannot match.
  4. Think beyond the conference. The firms reporting the best outcomes are those that used LEAP as an entry point and then maintained relationships, set up local offices, and pursued multi-year engagements.

From a product-building perspective, the Saudi market also creates a forcing function for quality. ZATCA compliance, data residency requirements, and enterprise-grade security expectations push Pakistani firms to build to a higher standard — and that standard transfers back to products sold in other markets.

What to Watch Next

  • Digital Nation Pakistan Act implementation: Watch for the Act’s legislative status, budget allocations, and the establishment of the central digital agency. Policy without implementation is the recurring failure mode in Pakistan’s technology governance.
  • PSEB LEAP 2026 outcomes data: If PSEB releases commercial outcome data for the 2026 delegation comparable to the $9.74 million reported for 2025, it will indicate whether the curated approach is working.
  • Riyadh office openings: Track which Pakistani firms move from attending to establishing physical presence. KAACIB is the first known example from this delegation.
  • Cybersecurity MoU terms: The specific scope of the Pakistan-Saudi cybersecurity cooperation — whether it includes joint capacity building, threat intelligence sharing, or product certification — will determine its practical impact.
  • 5G spectrum auction results: The auction was highlighted as a pillar of the digital stack. Its execution and the resulting improvement in connectivity infrastructure will be a concrete test of the government’s delivery capacity.
  • IT export trajectory: Pakistan’s IT exports reached $4.6 billion in FY2025-26, as covered in our previous analysis. Whether Saudi expansion contributes measurably to the next fiscal year’s numbers is worth tracking.

Conclusion

Pakistan’s presence at LEAP 2026 was not just another conference appearance. The combination of a national digital stack announcement, a curated business delegation with measurable prior outcomes, a cybersecurity MoU, and companies moving from attendance to physical office establishment suggests a deliberate strategy to build Pakistan’s technology export corridor with Saudi Arabia.

The risk is familiar: Pakistan’s technology policy history includes well-designed frameworks that struggled at the implementation stage. The INSPIRE semiconductor program completed its institutional structure recently, but the real test is whether it produces talent at scale. Similarly, the Digital Nation Pakistan Act and the national digital stack will be judged not by the keynote that announced them but by the infrastructure they deliver.

For founders and technology teams, the practical takeaway is narrower and more actionable: Saudi Arabia is an accessible, growing market for Pakistani technology products, LEAP is the most efficient entry point, and PSEB support is available. The firms that go with localized products and stay beyond the conference are the ones that convert presence into revenue.

Are you exploring Saudi expansion for your technology product? The combination of Vision 2030 demand and Pakistani technical capability is creating real opportunities — and the firms that move now will have first-mover advantage in a market that is actively building its digital infrastructure.
